# Annotation pilot: Haiku 5.5 against the gold set (13 Oct 2026)

**Question.** Is Claude Haiku 5.5 good enough to annotate the registry at Tier A, and how should it be
configured (effort, passes, consensus)? Is a bigger model worth it?

**Answer.** Yes, with two fixes in code. Use Haiku 5.5 at **high effort, focused mode** (one call per group of
sections). Sonnet 5.5 was no better at about 11 times the cost. The weak spots are relative dates (fixable in
code) and how finely obligations are split (a codebook convention), not the model.

## Setup

- **Gold set:** 26 Tier A annotations by Opus 5.5 in Claude Code (`annotations/`).
  - **Split** (`tools/pilot_split.json`): 13 laws to tune configurations and 13 held out for a final test.
  - **Composition:** each half has 9 "high-bite" laws (numeric thresholds, operative dates, private duties,
    penalties) and 4 thin controls.
  - **Not human-validated:** the gold set is itself machine output, so disagreements are not all Haiku errors
    (see below).
- **Runner:** `tools/annotate.py`.
  - The full annotation schema is too large for one structured-output call (grammar limit). So `packed` uses 4
    calls per law and `focused` 6.
  - Every output is validated. Failed quotes get one repair call: corrected, or the item is dropped.
- **Scorer:** `tools/score_pilot.py`.
  - Precision and recall per section, pooled across laws.
  - Thresholds are matched on value, events on type and date (gold `external` events excluded), and obligations
    on overlapping quotes. Provision IDs proved too inconsistent to match on.
- **Spend:** about $15.50 of API credit in total.

**Other findings:**
- **Low effort misses about half of thresholds and definitions,** and is not cheaper.
- **Quotes are not a problem:** nearly all are verbatim on the first pass, and the repair call fixes most of the
  rest.
- **Runs vary.** Two identical packed-high runs differed by up to 5–10 points on some sections. Small differences
  between arms are noise.
- **Packed mode truncates on long laws** (64K output-token limit at high effort: the EU Omnibus, the Cloud and AI
  Development Act, Argentina's Res. 264). Focused mode did not.
- **Cost depends mostly on length.** Laws under 30K tokens cost about $0.04 each, in either mode. Laws over 80K
  tokens cost $0.60–0.90, because codebook plus text crosses 100K tokens, where Haiku's price is 5 times higher.

## Why the numbers understate quality

A manual review of threshold disagreements (EnEfG; Argentina's Res. 264) found mostly convention differences:
- Gold coded technical grid-connection specs (voltage, frequency) as thresholds; Haiku did not.
- Haiku coded price surcharges as thresholds; gold coded them as enforcement.
- Haiku coded national energy-saving targets as thresholds, which codebook v1.2 says are government duties (a real
  Haiku error).

**Events:** gold computed relative dates ("24 months after publication") from known anchors. Haiku records the same
events but leaves the date empty, so they don't count as matches (e.g., Chile: 9 such events).

**Obligations:** recall plateaus near 65% in every arm, Sonnet included. Gold splits duties by paragraph, while
Haiku often groups them by article.

## Decisions

1. **Model and effort:** Haiku 5.5 at high effort. Sonnet is not used.
2. **Mode:** focused for every law. It is about as cheap as packed on short laws, has better threshold and
   definition recall in tuning, and never truncates.
3. **Chunk long texts** so each prompt stays under 100K tokens (document at most about 80K tokens, by chapter). This
   cuts long-law cost about 5 times and avoids truncation.
4. **Compute relative dates in code** (resolve stage): when an event's anchor (publication, entry into force) has a
   known date, compute the date from the offset in `date_text`. This addresses the main event gap.
5. **Codebook v1.3 clarifications:**
   - Technical performance specs count as thresholds only when they determine scope, eligibility or a legal
     consequence.
   - Price surcharges are `pricing_tier` thresholds, with the sanction regime in enforcement.
   - Obligations are one row per paragraph-level duty.
6. **Consensus (3 runs)** improves precision, but costs about 2.5 times focused mode. Keep it for the top Tier A
   laws only, if spot-checks show that precision matters there.
7. **Production:** use the Batch API (−50%).
   - **Estimate:** about $0.04 per short law, and about $0.15–0.20 per chunked long law, before the batch discount.
     For about 1,100 Tier A and B laws, that is roughly $35–45 with batching.
   - **Human spot-checks:** threshold values, obligation types and computed dates.

## Addendum (13 Oct 2026, evening): v1.3 runner

Implemented decisions 2–5:
- **Defaults:** focused mode, high effort.
- **Per-section instructions** (`GUIDANCE` in `tools/annotate.py`).
- **Long texts:** split at headings into parts of at most 70K tokens. A call whose answer hits the output limit is
  re-split, at most twice.
- **Relative dates:** event fields `offset_value`, `offset_unit`, `anchor_event` (schema 1.3); dates are computed in
  code.
- **Codebook v1.3.**

Scorer fixes:
- **Dates** match at their common precision.
- **Obligations** match on the same passage, or on the same article and obligation type.

Rescored with these fixes, the earlier `tune_focused_high` run reaches 90% / 88% (precision / recall) on events, and
obligations come out around 68–73% / 67–72%.

**First v1.3 checks:**
- **Italy:** event recall rose from 11% to 75%.
- **Peru:** 15 relative dates computed.
- **Chile:** "one row per paragraph-level duty" produced 209 obligation rows (gold: 39), about 117K output tokens and
  $0.20. The rule was changed to **one row per article, addressee role and obligation type**. A Chile rerun with
  that rule was still running at handoff (`build/pilot/v13b_check/`).

**Still to do before rollout:** rerun the 13 test laws with the final v1.3 runner and compare with
`test_focused_high` (`python tools/score_pilot.py build/pilot/test_focused_high build/pilot/test_v13`). If
obligation output is still very large, run the obligations group at medium effort.

**Chile rerun with the article-level rule:** 116 obligation rows (gold 39). The call still truncated and was
re-split ($0.19). Obligations scored 29% precision / 87% recall; events recall rose from 10% to 40%. Next: medium
effort for the obligations group and/or a cap on rows per article, then the test-set comparison.

## Confirmation run (13–14 Oct 2026): final v1.3 settings

Held-out test set, 13 laws (precision / recall):

| | earlier runner (focused, high) | v1.3, all high | v1.3, obligations at medium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dated events | 72 / 57 | 75 / 77 | 70 / 69 |
| Obligations (match) | 69 / 71 | 52 / 75 | 64 / 75 |
| Threshold values | 85 / 77 | 70 / 70 | 69 / 76 |
| Enforcement | 72 / 73 | 68 / 68 | 67 / 68 |
| Definitions | 83 / 62 | 79 / 71 | 77 / 68 |
| Cost | $2.26 | $1.64 | $1.97 |

- **Events** gain about 20 points of recall from the computed relative dates.
- **Obligations at medium effort** keep the recall of high effort with 12 points more precision, so medium is now
  the default for that group (`--group-effort obligations=medium`).
- **The threshold drop is one law.** The BIS AI diffusion rule (310K characters, 34 gold thresholds) was split in
  two parts and lost context at the cut; the other laws match or beat the earlier runner. A rerun with larger parts
  recovered part of it (value matches 21 → 25 of 34; earlier runner 29). Long, table-heavy rules remain the weak
  case.
- **Token estimate was too low.** Haiku 5.5 counts about 2.5 characters per token on legal text (the codebook is
  about 10K tokens, not 6K), so some "70K-token" parts were over 100K real tokens and billed at 5×: BIS cost $0.62
  instead of about $0.19. `est_tokens` now assumes 2.5 characters per token and parts are 80K estimated tokens.
- **Batch mode** (`--batch`) reproduced sync results on a two-law smoke test at half price.
